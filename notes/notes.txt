Note to self:  When you have a paragraph, and multiple lines, what if you have a line that 
hasn't yet had its 10 chars of text removed, when the 10 chars have already been removed 
from the paragraph.  If the line "refers" to its text in the paragraph, and the paragraph 
has already been updated, where does the text reside?  For the line. 

In the piece of work.  All deletions comes with the corresponding undo operation. 
Insertion, in this case.  The text for the insertion is in the item of work. 

So the line, and the item of work together, comprise the current (full) state 
of the line. 

The work item represents time.  The piece that is missing in current time, 
is still preserve in the bubble of past time, captured or represented by 
the item of work. 

The time of the paragraph (current)
The time of the line (past)
The time of the work item (bridges between past and current)

Apply the redo (or in this case, the do) brings the line from the past 
to the present (the current).  Applying the undo brings the line from 
current to past.  All data needs for the transformation has to reside 
in the piece of work. 

There should also be a piece of work associated with the paragraph. 
Forward and back. 

For a while, the "start here and have this length" of the line will 
be wrong.  It will not correspond to the present paragraph. 

Perhaps the lines should be changed instantly.  And the items of 
work represent a time lag, or a distance difference, between the 
client view and the server view. 

And perhaps they shouldn't be associated with 10-line blocks of lines. 
But should be free-floating.  The work item can address the lines 
(in the client). 

It has to be a work-item anyway.  Because it has to be shipped 
to the client. 

(and the client decides whether it another ideogram going down, 
or another letter going right?)
